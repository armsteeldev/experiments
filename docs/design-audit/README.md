# Experiments: Visual Excellence & Design Audit

## Baseline

**Repository state:** `improvement/accessible-depth` at `de6ef29` (`Improve accessibility across experiments`), clean working tree before this report. The current registry contains 13 exhibits.

**Rendered inspection:** local Vite app at 1440×900 and 390×844 in a separate headless Chrome session. I captured the homepage, collection, every exhibit, control panels, and selected first interactions. All 13 exhibit routes rendered at both sizes without horizontal viewport overflow. The capture set is in [`screenshots/baseline`](screenshots/baseline): [desktop overview](screenshots/baseline/desktop-overview.png), [mobile overview](screenshots/baseline/mobile-overview.png), [interaction overview](screenshots/baseline/interaction-overview.png), and focused captures for [the homepage](screenshots/baseline/home-desktop.png), [the loaded collection feature](screenshots/baseline/gallery-after-preview.png), [Fluid](screenshots/baseline/fluid-desktop.png), [Fluid after 15 seconds](screenshots/baseline/fluid-15s.png), [Reaction Diffusion after 15 seconds](screenshots/baseline/reaction-15s.png), [mobile Automata](screenshots/baseline/automata-mobile.png), and [mobile Adder](screenshots/baseline/adder-mobile.png).

The in-app browser is disabled in Work settings. I used the installed local headless Chrome through its debugging protocol for these rendered captures and scripted clicks. This is actual browser rendering, but it is not a visible-device or physical-touch session. The Infinite Shore reported its CPU progressive renderer and “WebGPU unavailable.” A direct renderer query on the Fluid canvas returned `ANGLE / Vulkan / SwiftShader`, and `navigator.gpu.requestAdapter()` returned `null`; Fluid and Reaction Diffusion therefore rendered through software WebGL in this session. Chrome also emitted a separate NVIDIA adapter line at shutdown, but no application WebGPU adapter or physical-GPU render path was available to the canvas. The software renderer logged GPU stalls. I do not treat those logs as evidence of hardware performance.

For the interaction pass I activated the first-action prompt on the exhibits that expose one, zoomed the fractal, dragged the attractor view, stepped the computer, trained the network, placed a force charge, started the glider, launched a body, and completed the Adder’s default 5 + 3 calculation. The latter showed decimal 8, binary 1000, carry 0. I also opened the shared controls panel on each route. The scripted Logic Laboratory input lookup did not match a switch, so that particular baseline input toggle was not verified. Emulated mobile screenshots verify layout only; they do not establish physical touch feel.

## Executive assessment

The project already has unusually strong foundations for an independent computational-art collection. It has 13 real, distinct simulations; the Adder and Inside the Machine make their underlying state legible; Weather, Remembered and Invisible Forces turn mathematical structure into coherent compositions; and the shared shell lets a visitor move between exhibits without interrupting direct manipulation. The current accessibility work improves first actions and explains technical parameters in plain language.

The project is polished, but it is not yet an exceptional visual experience. Its strongest obstacle is uneven exhibit output: A Quiet Current opens as a soft, nearly symmetrical ring of glowing beads, while The Pattern Beneath fills the screen with high-luminance electric green. Cellular Automata is a tiny cluster in a mostly empty field, especially on mobile. These are core artwork problems, not panel styling problems.

The surrounding identity is coherent but too dependent on a familiar dark-navy, pale-serif, italic-emphasis, tiny-monospace, translucent-pill vocabulary. The homepage is mostly large type over a faint cloudy field; the actual computational art first appears after scrolling. The collection preview becomes striking once its worker finishes, but its first capture was nearly blank until it had more time to render. Across exhibits, 6–9px uppercase monospace labels and repeated glass panels contribute to a technical mood but often make it look like a polished dashboard system rather than a singular digital-art environment.

Strong work worth preserving includes the composed attractor linework, the electrostatic field geometry, the central Adder bit-slice lesson, the live neural decision map, the fluid solver itself, and the procedural fern geometry. The improvement pass should raise the weaker default compositions and improve the opening art direction while preserving immediate simulation response and the explanations that genuinely help a first-time visitor.

**The two checklists and exhibit table below record the pre-change baseline.** Final changes, post-change verification, and remaining weaknesses are recorded at the end.

## Checklist A — UX & animation principles

| # | Principle | Status | Evidence and appropriate action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squash & stretch | Good | Canvas manipulation responds directly; deformation would misrepresent the simulations. Buttons mostly change color/border rather than compress. Keep simulation feedback immediate; add only a restrained pressed state to discrete controls if it reads clearly. |
| 2 | Anticipation wind-up | Good | The shared dock opens panels with a short directional transition, and the Adder frames a calculation before resolving signals. Continuous simulations do not delay or wind up pointer input. Preserve this split. |
| 3 | Staging the focus | Needs improvement | Attractors, Forces, and the Adder establish a readable focal object. Fluid’s default vortex and Reaction’s full-screen neon texture compete with the hint and controls; Automata’s focal seed is too small. Recompose those defaults before adding motion. |
| 4 | Pose to pose | Good | Adder has readable input, bit-slice, signal, and result states; Inside the Machine has fetch/decode/execute steps. Other studies are continuous simulations, for which fixed poses are not appropriate. Preserve the live behavior. |
| 5 | Follow through | Good | Gravity trails and the simulations’ own inertia provide follow-through. UI transitions are short and settle without bounce. Avoid adding decorative settling to direct manipulation. |
| 6 | Slow in & slow out | Good | Panel and page movement uses short custom cubic-bezier curves; hover transitions are around 0.18–0.25 seconds. The simulation canvases do not ease the pointer itself. Keep timings restrained. |
| 7 | Moving in arcs | Good | Orbital motion in Gravity and the trajectories in Weather come from their systems, not arbitrary UI paths. Panning and zooming stay direct. No extra arc animation is needed. |
| 8 | Secondary action | Good | Subtle ambient movement and the first-action prompts support the main exhibit without competing in the stronger scenes. The grain and aura should remain subordinate to the artwork. |
| 9 | Snappy timing | Good | Direct canvas gestures and parameter changes apply immediately; the Adder’s 270 ms illustrative signal timing remains controllable and does not block stepping or pausing. Preserve this behavior. |
| 10 | Exaggerated feedback | Good | Logic wires illuminate by value, Adder carries and outputs resolve visibly, and the computer highlights active steps. Fluid’s bright injected spot is visible but needs better surrounding visual material, not stronger feedback. |
| 11 | Solid drawing in 3D | Not applicable | The project has no spatial 3D renderer. Gravity and Attractors are 2D projections and read coherently as such. Do not introduce fake perspective to satisfy this item. |
| 12 | Overall appeal | Needs improvement | Five exhibits have especially strong visual output, but Fluid, Reaction, and Automata lower the collection’s baseline. The homepage also withholds the project’s strongest visual material. Improve the artwork and entry composition. |
| 13 | Fitts’s law | Needs improvement | Desktop dock buttons are about 42 px, but the mobile dock uses 34×38 px buttons and the prompt actions are 36 px high. Increase the most frequent touch targets where the dock can accommodate them; keep the layout within narrow screens. |
| 14 | Bigger hit areas | Needs improvement | The icon-only mobile dock and prompt close/reopen controls are smaller than a comfortable touch target. Preserve the icons but enlarge their hit boxes, especially for Controls, About, Pause, and Fullscreen. |
| 15 | Hick’s law | Good | Most advanced controls are hidden in the panel; the Adder’s width/architecture choices are behind “More settings”; exhibit-level first actions offer one concrete starting point. Keep advanced choice layered this way. |
| 16 | Miller’s law | Needs improvement | The first actions group the basic action well, and Adder/CPU stages make complex state legible. Some control panels combine many sliders with repeated explanatory paragraphs and 7–10px labels. Trim duplicate notes and group related settings. |
| 17 | Doherty threshold | Needs improvement | UI state changes and tutorial actions respond promptly in the rendered browser. This pass cannot measure physical-GPU or mobile frame time; software WebGL produced readback stalls, and the collection preview needed additional time before the fractal was visible. Measure real hardware before claiming smooth performance. |
| 18 | Postel’s law | Good | Search normalizes diacritics and case; native range/select inputs constrain values; Adder inputs validate integer range; shared URLs are decoded into guarded defaults. Preserve these tolerant inputs and explicit invalid states. |
| 19 | Jakob’s law | Good | Navigation uses links and familiar buttons, range controls are native, and controls can be found through a labeled dock. Canvas drag/zoom is unconventional only where the artwork calls for it. Keep keyboard and browser conventions. |
| 20 | Frequency of use | Good | Repeated simulation gestures update immediately. Playback can be paused; reduced-motion preference initializes exhibits paused. Avoid looping flourish on controls or easing pointer-driven simulation state. |

## Checklist B — AI design-slop indicators

| # | Indicator | Status | Evidence and appropriate action |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tags & pills | Good | Gallery filters are mostly plain text; the dock is one capsule and the first-action prompt has a compact action button. These shapes serve navigation and grouping rather than decorating every label. Preserve selectively. |
| 2 | Excessive italics | Needs improvement | The entrance, Automata, Neural Networks, and Invisible Forces repeat the same editorial-serif plus italic phrase pattern. Keep the entrance’s emphasis, then give exhibit headings more varied typographic voices. |
| 3 | Instrument Serif | Needs improvement | Instrument Serif is not loaded, but the entrance explicitly prefers Bodoni 72 and Didot before the broader serif fallback. That high-contrast editorial display style is recognizable and familiar. It suits the project when used sparingly; let the exhibit art carry more of the identity. |
| 4 | Negative kerning | Needs improvement | Before the pass, the active hero rule used −0.067em desktop and −0.064em mobile tracking. The large headline remained readable, but the mobile treatment looked compressed. The revised entrance uses −0.035em; keep checking the other display headings for lost air. |
| 5 | Over-the-top detailed UI | Good | The Adder and machine controls are dense because they expose a real gate trace and programmable computer. Their advanced settings are secondary. Preserve the meaningful machinery; remove duplicated notes rather than flattening it. |
| 6 | Overusing dividers | Needs improvement | Thin rules recur in cards, panels, select rows, information sections, and docks. They are restrained individually but accumulate into a dashboard rhythm. Remove dividers that do not separate different kinds of information. |
| 7 | Color shades & opacities | Needs improvement | The shared UI uses many close alpha variants of blue-grey glass, while the exhibits use more saturated accents. Several labels depend on very low-opacity gray. Consolidate utility surfaces and improve microcopy contrast; retain exhibit-specific palettes. |
| 8 | Containers with borders | Needs improvement | The Adder’s board and circuit diagrams need boundaries, but collection cards, floated panels, info modules, and several control groups all repeat outlined surfaces. Let more artwork sit directly on the field; reserve borders for functional grouping. |
| 9 | Overly detailed diagrams | Good | Logic, Neural Networks, and Inside the Machine diagrams encode real state. Their complexity is instructional, not ornament. Keep the diagrams and improve labels or stage framing only where legibility suffers. |
| 10 | Verbose, redundant text | Needs improvement | First-action prompts are useful, but some exhibits repeat their teaching point in the prompt, stage caption, and multiple control notes. Fluid includes two overlapping explanations of simulation behavior. Keep one concise explanation at each level. |
| 11 | Code breadcrumbs | Needs improvement | `FLOATING-POINT VELOCITY`, grid dimensions, curve counts, render backend, and other small diagnostic labels appear over artwork. Keep live status that changes a decision; demote or remove ambient metadata that only signals “technical.” |
| 12 | Section numbering | Good | 01–13 identifies collection order and is repeated in route navigation. There is no unnecessary numbering of content sections. Preserve it. |
| 13 | Eyebrows | Needs improvement | Most exhibit headers begin with tiny uppercase category/kicker text; prompts and panels repeat the same treatment. Keep a few useful context labels, but vary or remove the rest. |
| 14 | Dithering | Needs improvement | `ambient-grain` adds a site-wide SVG noise layer and some scenes add dot grids. The grid is useful in stateful diagrams; page-wide film grain is subtle but currently generic. Retain only texture that improves a specific material. |
| 15 | ASCII images | Not applicable | No ASCII artwork is used. |
| 16 | Squircles | Good | Roundness is concentrated in the dock, a few icon controls, and the hint panel; exhibit boards remain square or softly rounded. The capsule dock is a clear shared navigation device, not a universal shape treatment. |
| 17 | Excessive mono fonts | Needs improvement | Monospace is used for most small status, category, dimension, and diagnostic labels across all routes. It suits values and technical notation, but its frequency makes unrelated labels look alike. Reserve it for numbers, symbols, and live readouts. |
| 18 | ALL CAPS subtitles | Needs improvement | Many labels are uppercase, widely tracked, and very small. They work as short instrument labels but become unreadable when used for longer captions. Use sentence case for supporting prose and reserve caps for brief controls. |
| 19 | Gradient backgrounds | Needs improvement | Gradients in the Adder board, electric field, and glass controls have a clear material function. The site-wide cloudy glow and repeated low-contrast gradient panels are less distinctive. Preserve exhibit lighting; reduce generic ambient washes. |
| 20 | Periods in titles | Good | The entrance line ends with a period because it is a complete sentence. Other exhibit headings do not add decorative terminal periods. Preserve natural punctuation. |

### Final assessment

The collection now opens with an artwork, and three weak defaults have more deliberate framing and color. Those changes improve the first impression and give Fluid and Automata a clearer focal shape. The project is still uneven: Reaction Diffusion remains deliberately full-bleed and busy; Fluid still looks softly diffused in the available software renderer; and several secondary exhibits retain small mobile labels, overlapping prompts, or a compact focal object. The overall dark glass, micro-monospace, and high-contrast serif language is coherent but still risks reading as a polished dashboard. I would describe the project as polished and distinctive in its strongest exhibits, with clear improvements, but not yet at the “exceptional” standard in the request.

The baseline review remains useful for nine unmodified exhibits: The Infinite Shore, A Murmuration, Weather, Remembered, A Grammar of Growth, Gravity Playground, Inside the Machine, Neural Networks, Logic Laboratory, and Invisible Forces. Their earlier opportunities remain open. In particular, the small mobile labels in Inside the Machine, the Neural hint over the top of its plot, and the fern prompt’s overlap with its plant still merit a separate targeted pass.

### Remaining design and technical work

1. Revisit Fluid’s display shader and pigment advection on a physical GPU. The seed now has a stronger shape, but the softened output still does not fully read as fine liquid ribbons.
2. Give Reaction Diffusion a calmer default density or a more deliberate localized opening seed while keeping the full-field modes available. Confirm the alternate CPU fallback on a device without WebGL.
3. Refine the mobile Adder stage composition further; the current pass only shortens the output path.
4. Address prompt placement in A Grammar of Growth and Neural Networks, then raise the smallest mobile labels and hit areas without shrinking the artwork.
5. Continue art-directing the system typography and glass surfaces. This pass changed the entrance’s composition and italic treatment but did not replace the serif/monospace vocabulary across all exhibits.

No dependencies were added. No push or deployment was performed.
