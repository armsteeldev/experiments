# The Invisible World · phase 2 review

This review records the browser-facing problems found during the second pass, the changes made, and the evidence used to check them. The six exhibits were reviewed in an isolated headless Chromium session at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844 CSS pixels. The browser used software rendering. These captures support visual/layout and interaction review; they do not establish physical-GPU or phone performance.

## Exhibit reviews

### 14 · The Wave Chamber

**What the browser review exposed.** The first field had only 192 × 120 cells, which softened narrow wavefronts and barrier detail. Its opening selected the double slit before the wavefront had crossed the barrier, leaving much of the composition empty and making the first result hard to read. The physical meaning of the energy display was not local to the sampled field, and the user needed a clear distinction between an absorbing edge and an ideal boundary.

**What changed.** The default field is now 288 × 180 cells and opens with two phase-locked sources so the subject named by the title and first interaction is visible immediately. The double-slit example remains available as a preset. A fourth preset builds a closed rectangular chamber, seeds its discrete Neumann standing mode and drives it at the frequency calculated from the finite-difference stencil; detuning changes how energy is stored. The stable leapfrog solver retains its 2D Courant bound, and the edge sponge and zero-normal-flux solid stencil are explicitly distinguished. The energy view colorizes a local estimate built from field velocity and spatial gradients; a probe samples the same quantity. Continuous emitters have bounded parameters and an eight-source work cap. Phase wander remains seeded so the coherence example is reproducible. The rendering keeps phase contours over the interference field rather than turning it into an undifferentiated glow.

**Evidence and limits.** Tests check propagation, bounded amplitude after 1,200 steps, the Courant threshold, solid-barrier isolation, superposition, deterministic phase diffusion, cavity response near versus away from a discrete resonance, probe bounds and emitter limits. The browser pass captures the opening, evolved field, changed source/barrier interaction, energy mode, double-slit, opposing standing-wave and resonant-chamber presets, and mobile layouts. See [opening](evidence/waves/01-opening-desktop.png), [evolved field](evidence/waves/02-evolved-desktop.png), [energy view](evidence/waves/04-advanced-desktop.png), [double slit](evidence/waves/09-double-slit-desktop.png), [standing mode](evidence/waves/10-standing-desktop.png), [resonant chamber](evidence/waves/08-resonant-chamber-desktop.png) and [mobile controls](evidence/waves/07-controls-mobile.png). The energy estimate is not a conserved total in this driven, damped discrete system; the sponge is not a perfectly matched layer. No WebGL solver was introduced because the bounded typed-array field met this exhibit's current visual and interaction needs in the reviewed environment.

### 15 · A Geometry of Light

**What the browser review exposed.** The opening lens had been aligned parallel to the incoming beam, so the visible component received no useful rays. Several small decorative markers implied transform handles but had no corresponding action. This weakened both the first composition and the promise of direct manipulation.

**What changed.** The default lens is oriented across the dispersed fan. Selection has a generous hit area and a clear selected state, and the actual lens body can be dragged and rotated; nonfunctional handle decorations were removed. The scene remains a 2D geometric optics model: exact ray/segment intersections, vector reflection and Snell refraction, wavelength-dependent prism dispersion and total internal reflection. The thin-lens approximation is identified as such.

**Evidence and limits.** A deterministic opening-scene check traces 80 rays across wavelengths and requires at least 60 to meet the lens; the current setup meets that threshold. Mathematical checks cover reflection, refraction, prism entry/exit and an off-axis converging lens ray. Browser review exercised the optical elements, presets, selection and mobile controls. See [opening](evidence/optics/01-opening-desktop.png), [manipulated scene](evidence/optics/03-interaction-desktop.png), [advanced controls](evidence/optics/04-advanced-desktop.png) and [mobile controls](evidence/optics/07-controls-mobile.png). Lens behavior is paraxial and thin; it does not model thick-lens aberrations, Fresnel intensity, polarization or diffraction.

### 16 · The Shape of Sound

**What the browser review exposed.** A single smooth tone did not immediately reveal why Fourier components matter. Audio could be made audible, but the system needed clear timbre starting points, a direct way to add and shape a partial, and an evolving view connected to the generated signal. Route exit also needed an explicit audio-resource check.

**What changed.** The instrument opens on a triangle wave and offers sine, triangle, square and saw presets using Fourier-series coefficients. Visitors can add/remove harmonics and adjust a selected partial's amplitude and phase, then inspect a one-period waveform, FFT or rolling spectrogram. The plotted components apply the same low-pass filter response used in the audio path. Playback remains visitor-initiated, parameter changes are smoothed, output is capped at 5.5% gain, repeated play/mute/stop reuses one context, and leaving the route stops the oscillator and closes the context.

**Evidence and limits.** Numerical checks validate harmonic bins and phases, preset coefficients, filter response and the gain bound. Browser instrumentation verifies the initial muted state, start/mute/unmute/stop behavior, a single reused `AudioContext`, mobile interaction and context closure after route exit. See [opening](evidence/sound/01-opening-desktop.png), [spectrum interaction](evidence/sound/03-interaction-desktop.png), [waveform and spectrogram](evidence/sound/04-advanced-desktop.png) and [mobile controls](evidence/sound/07-controls-mobile.png). These checks do not establish pleasantness on headphones. I did not perform human listening evaluation. The synth is a harmonic periodic oscillator rather than an anti-aliased wavetable instrument; high partials are constrained by the supported fundamental range but this is not a general-purpose audio synthesizer.

### 17 · The Living Garden

**What the browser review exposed.** The earlier opening looked like similar glowing points and did not make inherited traits or environmental pressure easy to read. The original short browser observation was far too brief to assess reproduction, mortality or population recovery. Resource collection and faster movement also needed an explicit energetic tradeoff so one visible trait was not simply a free advantage.

**What changed.** Organisms now differ in silhouette, hue and movement according to their traits. Food seeking, movement, metabolism, conversion efficiency and reproduction all participate in bounded energy accounting; offspring inherit traits with bounded mutation. A seasonal control cycles between bloom and drought conditions, the interface shows live climate and trait summaries, and a small population/efficiency plot exposes longer changes. Experiments can be seeded, saved and restored. Population, food and history have explicit caps.

**Long-run evidence.** Deterministic accelerated runs covered 37,000 steps: 8,000 resource-rich, 8,000 resource-poor, 10,000 alternating cool bloom/hot drought, 3,000 recovery after an eight-agent decline, and an 8,000-step crowded-resource stress run starting at 300 agents. The measured outcomes were:

| Condition | Final population | Recorded range | Generation | Births / deaths |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Resource-rich, seed 41721 | 149 | 139–158 | 29 | 2,418 / 2,303 |
| Resource-poor, seed 991 | 19 | 17–29 | 33 | 544 / 559 |
| Alternating climate, seed 8127 | 44 | 26–138 | 40 | 1,854 / 1,844 |
| Recovery from 8, seed 44 | 122 | — | 14 | 851 / 737 |
| Crowded-resource stress, seed 66291, start 300 | 177 | 166–190 recent; 20–320 overall | 28 | 2,754 / 2,877 |

No run exceeded the 320-agent or 150-food caps; the crowded stress run reached the population cap exactly, then fell to 20 during resource competition and recovered to 177. All 37,000 accelerated model steps took about 1.1 seconds in Node in this run; this is not a browser-frame or mobile benchmark. A clean-process 18-second browser sample grew from 34 to 48 agents. After forced V8 collection, listener count returned from 207 to 177; eight further remounts held at 177 listeners and 266 DOM nodes after the first cycle. Chromium heap estimates started at 4.58 MiB, reached 5.51 MiB by the eighth remount and fluctuated around 5.5 MiB over the last cycles; this short sample suggests warm-up and a plateau but cannot establish long-term bounded memory. The same software-rendered browser recorded 33.4 ms median and 50.1 ms 95th-percentile animation-frame intervals. See [opening](evidence/garden/01-opening-desktop.png), [evolved garden](evidence/garden/02-evolved-desktop.png), [traits and season controls](evidence/garden/04-advanced-desktop.png), [near-cap browser interaction](evidence/garden/08-high-population-desktop.png) and [mobile interaction](evidence/garden/06-interaction-mobile.png). This remains a small teaching ecology, not a validated account of real-world evolution; it has no predation and deliberately simplified resource and inheritance rules.

### 18 · The Living Network

**What the browser review exposed.** The prior opening began with traffic already in flight, so a visitor could see motion before learning how to initiate a message. The route itself was not demonstrated as a complete action from selecting endpoints through delivery. Queue changes also created a risk that a packet could revisit a previous node.

**What changed.** The exhibit opens with a quiet graph. Selecting one node and then another sends a message directly; the route, packet movement, delivery/drop counters and failed links are visible. The tool row and live counters now sit in the open left column, away from the right-side controls panel. It supports topology edits, link failure, capacity and traffic changes. Each packet's next-hop route excludes nodes it has already visited, which prevents queue-cost changes from producing backtracking loops. Active packets and per-link queues are bounded.

**Evidence and limits.** Tests cover deterministic shortest-path routing, link failure/recovery, delivery, queue overflow, disconnected networks, self-delivery, invalid endpoints and no-backtracking under changing queue costs. The actual browser pass selected source and destination, then recorded six delivered messages at tick 91 with zero in flight and zero dropped; it also exercised disruption and recovery. See [opening](evidence/network/01-opening-desktop.png), [interaction](evidence/network/03-interaction-desktop.png), [delivered messages](evidence/network/08-delivered-desktop.png), [advanced controls](evidence/network/04-advanced-desktop.png) and [mobile controls](evidence/network/07-controls-mobile.png). This is a discrete teaching network, not a model of TCP, congestion control or Internet-scale routing.

### 19 · Where Light Falls

**What the browser review exposed.** In the first pass, the prominent animated disk/halo could read as the black-hole calculation even though its relationship to the computed photon trajectories was weak. The source projection also wrapped pixels across opposite image edges, creating false continuity. The ray-path and sky composition needed separate, defensible visual conventions.

**What changed.** The ray-path view is now drawn from integrated equatorial Schwarzschild null geodesics. It masks the physical `2M` event horizon and marks the `3M` photon sphere; captured paths terminate before the mask. The lensed-sky view uses a radial source-plane projection driven by the computed Schwarzschild deflection table, bilinearly samples the source and no longer wraps the image at its edges. The artistic disk and rings appear only in the sky view and are labeled as not radiative transfer. The per-exhibit worker is terminated on unmount and its error fallback timer is cancelled.

**Evidence and limits.** Tests compare weak-field bending with `4M/b`, halve the RK4 angular step, check mass scaling and monitor the conserved impact invariant; a subcritical truncated path is not called captured unless it actually reaches the horizon. Raster dimensions and integration ranges are bounded. Browser interaction switches between sky and ray-path views, varies mass/observer/impact parameter, selects a path on the canvas and opens the mathematical description. See [opening sky](evidence/lensing/01-opening-desktop.png), [interaction](evidence/lensing/03-interaction-desktop.png), [captured trajectory and model](evidence/lensing/04-advanced-desktop.png) and [mobile controls](evidence/lensing/07-controls-mobile.png). The sky is a thin-lens mapping of a synthetic source plane driven by numerically integrated deflections; it is not full finite-camera general-relativistic ray tracing or radiative transfer. The model is non-rotating Schwarzschild, not a Kerr black hole. Specialist physics and art review remain outstanding.

## Verification record

- `npm run test:invisible-world`: solver/model edge cases, stability, deterministic seeded ecology, accelerated populations, audio/Fourier signal behavior, routing and geodesic reference checks.
- `node scripts/qa-invisible-world.mjs`: 50 screenshots; six exhibits; desktop and mobile layouts; direct interactions and wave presets; a paused 320-agent garden render; audio lifecycle; message delivery; 12 repeated SPA entries; 19-item gallery and collection search; zero captured console/HTTP errors. Chromium 157 headless, software renderer.
- `npm run qa:existing`: route and canvas regression check of the original 13 exhibits at desktop and mobile sizes.
- `npm run qa:resources`: clean-process 18-second garden browser sample, 120-frame interval sample and eight route remounts. After forced collection, listeners stayed at 177 and nodes at 266 across the last seven remounts; used heap fluctuated around 5.5 MiB in the last four. Frame intervals were 33.4 ms median / 50.1 ms p95 in software-rendered Chromium. These are short-run process estimates, not device-wide memory or physical-GPU performance.
- `npm run verify`: production build, existing and new model checks, and site metadata/sitemap checks.

The screenshot set is in [`evidence/`](evidence/); the machine-readable route summary is [`run-summary.json`](evidence/run-summary.json), and the short resource sample is [`runtime-resource-samples.json`](evidence/runtime-resource-samples.json). No human usability study, human listening session, real mobile-device run, physical-GPU benchmark or independent specialist scientific review was performed. The local changes and evidence are not a release: no merge, push or deployment occurred.
